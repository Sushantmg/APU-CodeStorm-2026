Event overview

About APU CodeStorm 2026

A 48-hour interfaculty hackathon organised by students, for students.

Purpose

Why CodeStorm exists

APU CodeStorm 2026 is an imaginary student event created for this project. In the story of the site, the hackathon exists because students across faculties rarely get a shared, deadline-driven space to build something real together. Programming students travel in circles of code, business students in circles of strategy, and design students in circles of interfaces - a hackathon forces those circles to overlap for 48 hours.

The platform you are reading serves both participants and non-participants: registrants use it to plan their weekend, while visitors use it to understand what the event is, when it happens and how to follow it remotely.

Background

From a notice board to a platform

The first imagined edition in 2023 ran with a printed poster and a spreadsheet. Feedback from that edition identified three recurring problems: schedules changed without notice, rules were scattered across chat groups, and volunteers had no single source of truth.

CodeStorm 2026 answers all three. The programme page publishes a filterable timetable with a downloadable calendar, the rulebook lives in a single resources library, and volunteer coordination runs through the same registration flow used by participants.

Learning objectives

What the event promises participants

1. Ship under pressure

Teams must produce a working, demoable prototype in 48 hours with a fixed scope and a hard deadline.

2. Work across disciplines

Cross-faculty teams mirror real product squads, so every member contributes a different discipline.

3. Present with clarity

Each team delivers a three-minute pitch plus a five-minute Q&A before a panel of academics and industry judges.

4. Learn from mentors

Twenty-four mentors run scheduled clinics on architecture, user experience, data ethics and pitching.

5. Document the process

Teams keep a build log, which doubles as portfolio evidence after the event.

6. Compete fairly

Published rubrics, code-freeze rules and plagiarism checks keep the playing field level.

Programme at a glance

How the weekend unfolds

  • Day 1 · Friday 4 December, 09:00

    Registration, kickoff keynote and team formation

    Problem statements are released at 10:30; solo participants are matched into teams before lunch.

  • Day 1 · Friday, 14:00

    Build sprint I

    Mentors rotate through tables; architecture clinic at 16:00.

  • Day 2 · Saturday 5 December, 10:00

    Build sprint II and design critique

    Mid-build review with judges is optional but strongly encouraged.

  • Day 3 · Sunday 6 December, 09:00

    Code freeze, submissions and demo day

    Submissions close at 12:00; pitching runs 14:00–17:00; awards at 17:30.

Governance

Rules that keep it fair

Eligibility

  • Open to all currently enrolled APU students, any faculty and any intake.
  • Teams of 3–4 members; solo hackers are matched at the formation session.
  • At least one member must be a first- or second-year student.
  • Each student may register for only one team.

Integrity

  • All code must be written during the event, except approved libraries and templates.
  • Teams may use open-source libraries, public APIs and pre-trained models with credit.
  • Plagiarism or undisclosed generated content leads to disqualification.
  • Final source code and a build log must be submitted before the code freeze.
